\## Phishing Case Study



Phishing is a form of cybercrime where attackers impersonate trusted entities to trick users into revealing sensitive information such as login credentials, OTPs, or banking details. These attacks rely more on human psychology—such as urgency and familiarity—than technical complexity.



A common scenario involves a student receiving an email that appears to be from their university or bank, often with a subject like “Urgent: Account Verification Required.” The attacker first imitates a trusted source, then creates urgency to push the user into acting quickly. The email contains a link that redirects the user to a fake website that closely resembles the original. When the user enters their credentials or OTP, the attacker captures this information and may use it for unauthorized access or financial transactions.



Students are frequent targets due to high digital activity and quick response behavior. The consequences can include account compromise, data theft, and financial loss.



To better understand this, I also developed an interactive visual novel simulating phishing scenarios. It required users to analyze emails and make decisions, demonstrating how small lapses in judgment can lead to serious consequences. This highlights that awareness and careful decision-making are the most effective defenses against phishing.
